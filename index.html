01 — UNDERSTAND THE ISSUE

What is cybercrime?

Knowing how online threats work is the first step toward preventing harm.

⌘

Crime involving computers, networks, or digital systems

Cybercrime refers broadly to unlawful acts in which computers, devices, networks, or digital data are the target, the tool, or an important part of the offense. Examples include unauthorized access, data interference, online fraud, identity misuse, and certain forms of online abuse.

Some harmful online conduct is addressed by other laws as well. The exact legal classification depends on the facts and applicable Philippine law.

A

How it evolved

As computers became connected through the internet, threats expanded from isolated system intrusions to phishing, account compromise, ransomware, payment fraud, and large-scale data theft.

B

Why criminals do it

Common motives include financial gain, stealing identities or information, extortion, disruption, espionage, revenge, and gaining unauthorized access.

IndividualsFinancial loss, privacy invasion, stress, and identity misuse.
BusinessesOperational disruption, data exposure, reputational damage, and recovery costs.
SchoolsDisrupted learning, compromised student records, and unsafe online environments.
GovernmentService disruption, exposed records, and reduced public trust.

03 — SPOT THE DECEPTION

Common online scams

Scammers often combine believable stories with pressure to make you act before checking.

01

Online shopping scams

Fake sellers, copied product photos, unusually low prices, or requests to pay outside a trusted platform.

02

Investment & cryptocurrency scams

Promises of guaranteed or unusually high returns, fake dashboards, referral pressure, or demands for extra fees to withdraw.

03

Romance scams

A person builds an online relationship and later invents an emergency or investment opportunity requiring money.

04

Job recruitment scams

Fake employers ask for upfront fees, sensitive documents, or deposits before a real hiring process.

05

Text, SMS & email scams

Messages impersonate banks, delivery firms, government offices, or service providers and include malicious links.

06

Fake giveaways & lottery scams

You are told you won a prize you never entered, then asked to pay a fee or disclose personal details.

07

Social media & impersonation scams

Fake profiles or compromised accounts ask friends for emergency money, codes, or suspicious links.

08

Loan & QR-code scams

Fake lenders demand advance fees; malicious QR codes may lead to lookalike payment or login pages.

THE SCAMMER'S PLAYBOOK

Watch for these red flags.

  • “Act now or your account will be closed.”
  • Guaranteed profit or a risk-free investment.
  • Requests for OTPs, PINs, or passwords.
  • Payment to a personal account or unfamiliar wallet.
  • Refusal to let you verify independently.
  • Unexpected links, attachments, or QR codes.

Pause. Do not reply, click, pay, or share a code until you independently verify the request.

04 — BUILD YOUR DEFENSE

Cybersecurity habits

Small, consistent actions can make it harder for attackers to access your accounts and information.

01

Use strong, unique passwords

Use long passwords or passphrases that are different for every important account. A password manager can help you store them securely.

02

Turn on two-factor authentication

Enable MFA/2FA wherever available. Prefer an authenticator app or passkey when supported; never share one-time codes.

03

Check links before opening

Inspect the actual domain, watch for misspellings, and reach services through a saved bookmark or official app instead of message links.

04

Limit personal information

Review privacy settings and avoid publicly posting addresses, schedules, ID details, account numbers, or security answers.

05

Bank safely

Use official banking apps and trusted networks. Set transaction alerts, verify recipients, and contact your bank immediately about suspicious activity.

06

Secure social accounts

Use unique passwords, enable login alerts, review active sessions, and verify unusual requests through another communication channel.

07

Download carefully

Install apps from official stores or vendor sites. Avoid pirated software, unknown attachments, and unexpected browser extensions.

08

Update and back up

Install security updates promptly. Keep a separate, tested backup of important files to reduce the impact of ransomware or device failure.

09

Use safer connections

Avoid sensitive transactions on public Wi-Fi. Use your mobile data or a trusted network; HTTPS alone does not prove a site is legitimate.

✦
CYBER TIP OF THE MOMENT

Never share your OTP—even with someone claiming to be from your bank.

IF YOU SUSPECT A SCAM

What to do next

  1. Stop communication. Do not click more links, send money, or share codes.
  2. Contact your bank or e-wallet immediately using the number or support channel published in its official app or website. Ask about securing the account and disputing unauthorized transactions.
  3. Secure your accounts. Change compromised passwords from a trusted device, revoke unknown sessions, and enable MFA.
  4. Preserve evidence. Keep screenshots, full message details, sender numbers, URLs, account names, transaction references, dates, and receipts. Do not edit originals.
  5. Report to the appropriate authority. Use official CICC, PNP ACG, NBI, or NPC channels depending on the incident. Ask the agency what additional documents it needs.

Important: Do not post private evidence publicly. Never send passwords, OTPs, or full payment-card details to an unverified contact. In immediate danger, contact local emergency services or police.

Source note: Agency links above point to official domains. Reporting procedures, contact details, and jurisdiction may change; verify them directly before acting. This site is an educational resource, not an official government service.
07 — QUICK ANSWERS

Frequently asked questions

Simple answers to common questions about digital safety.

What should I do if I clicked a suspicious link?

Close the page. Do not enter information or download anything. If you entered a password, change it from a trusted device and sign out other sessions. If you entered banking details or an OTP, contact your bank immediately. Run a security scan if a file was downloaded or installed.

Is every unexpected text message a scam?

Not necessarily, but treat unexpected messages cautiously. Do not use the link or phone number in the message to verify it. Contact the organization using details from its official website, app, or a trusted statement.

Can a legitimate-looking website be fake?

Yes. Criminals can copy logos, layouts, and branding. Check the complete domain carefully and access services through a known bookmark or official app. A padlock or HTTPS connection does not guarantee that a website is trustworthy.

What is an OTP and should I share it?

An OTP is a one-time password or verification code used to confirm a login or transaction. Never share it with another person. A genuine support representative should not ask you to disclose your password, PIN, or OTP.

What evidence should I keep when reporting a scam?

Keep original messages, screenshots, sender details, URLs, profile links, transaction receipts and reference numbers, dates and times, and any communication with the bank or platform. Preserve files without editing them and follow the investigator's instructions.

Does RA 10175 cover every harmful online action?

No. RA 10175 addresses specified cybercrime offenses. Other conduct may be covered by other Philippine laws, depending on the facts. For legal advice or a formal complaint, consult an appropriate authority or qualified lawyer.

Is public Wi-Fi always unsafe?

Not every public Wi-Fi network is malicious, but you cannot always verify who operates it or who else is connected. Avoid sensitive transactions on untrusted networks; use mobile data or a trusted connection when possible.

Will this website report a scam for me?

No. This is an educational demo website. Its feedback form does not submit to a server, and it does not contact law enforcement. Use the official agency channels listed in the Philippine resources section.
